# Bell Test Lab — Round 3 First-Time-Visitor Review

*Reviewed cold from all 21 screenshots in `screenshots/r3/` (`00-first` through `09-teacher-mode` across desktop `1920×1080`, phone `390×844 @2x`, and projector `1280×720`), wearing first the hat of a documentary-watching adult with no physics background and then a high-school physics teacher planning a classroom projection.*

## 1. Scores (1–5, strict: 4 = genuinely good, 5 = award-level)

- **Clarity: 3** — Opening hook and Steps 1–3 are remarkably clear with zero surface jargon, but Step 4's clipped angle labels (`20°`), the abrupt jump from 3-dial magnets to a 0/1 game, Step 5's `76.3%` score contradicting a clamped 75% wall, and Step 7's frosted wall placed *beside* rather than *in front of* Alice create real mid-story friction.
- **Ease: 4** — Consistently big tap targets, bottom-anchored gold CTAs, always-visible `← Back` / `Start over` / sound controls, and zero modals or walls of instructions make progression genuinely effortless (only phone bottom-sheet height on Steps 5 and 7 feels tight).
- **Beauty: 3** — The dark-velvet studio lighting, brushed-brass hardware, and calm typography on desktop `00`–`02` clearly beat Quantum Flytrap and fun-with-quantum, yet cropped hero boxes on `00-first-phone`, soft/blurry 3D rendering on `01`–`09` phone, floating `▲/●` icons colliding with the score bar, clipped `120°/240°` labels, and matte grey shatter shards hold overall visual craft just below a clean 4.
- **Smoothness: 4** — Layout chrome and right-rail anchors stay rock-solid across steps with clear motion storytelling (flying motes, rotating dials, lever throw, shatter-to-settle arc), with only phone sheet-height jumps between steps detracting *(note: actual frame rate / FPS cannot be measured from software-rendered static screenshots)*.
- **Delight: 3** — The museum-hardware vibe, peel-off magnet reveal, physical brass lever, and Teacher voting gate show genuine craft, but the centerpiece Step 6 glass shatter looks more like matte grey origami triangles overlapping text (and a blurry grey puff on phone) than a gasp-worthy crystalline breakthrough.

## 2. Per-Screen Audit (00 → 09)

### `00-first-desk.png` & `00-first-phone.png` — Opening Hook
- **Understood in 5 seconds:** Two unconnected mystery boxes are receiving twin golden pulses from a central brass emitter and lighting up together. Single magnetic question (*"Two boxes. No wires. How do they always agree?"*) and one gold CTA (*"Investigate"*). Immediate curiosity, zero clutter.
- **Confusions / friction:** Mild teaser exaggeration — *"always agree"* is true only when dials match (both dials visibly point to `1`, which helps), whereas Step 2 reveals ~50% overall agreement.
- **Broken / cramped / cheap:** On **`00-first-phone.png`**, both the left and right boxes are **horizontally cropped** off the edges of the viewport while ~65% of the vertical frame is empty background/floor. Also, the step-progress dots visible at top center on desktop disappear on phone across all screens (acceptable for decluttering, though worth noting).

### `01-box-step-desk.png` & `01-box-step-phone.png` — Step 1: The Mystery Boxes
- **Understood in 5 seconds:** The boxes belong to `ALICE` and `BOB`, each has dials `1–2–3`, matching dials yield `100%` same colour (`50 / 50 — zero misses`), and Einstein suspected a pre-set "hidden card." Floating shape+colour rings (`●` Jade, `▲` Coral) make outcomes accessible without relying on hue alone.
- **Confusions / friction:** Minimal — this is a strong, welcoming first interaction.
- **Broken / cramped / cheap:** Starting here on phone (`01-box-step-phone.png` through `09-teacher-mode-phone.png`), the 3D stage renders noticeably **softer/blurrier** than `00-first-phone.png` and the crisp UI bottom sheet below it; dial numbers and plaques look fuzzy. Also, the lamp domes themselves glow very pale (almost white-mint / pale cream) compared to the richer floating icons above them.

### `02-random-tally-desk.png` & `02-random-tally-phone.png` — Step 2: Random Dials
- **Understood in 5 seconds:** With random dials over 1,000 pairs, Alice and Bob match ~50% of the time (`49.1%` desk, `49.2%` phone). The gold-bordered *"Here's the puzzle"* callout cleanly sets up why 100%-when-matched + 50%-overall is surprisingly hard to fake with a hidden card.

### `03-card-grid-desk.png` & `03-card-grid-phone.png` — Step 3: Test a Hidden Card

## 3. Extra Checks for Round 3

### A. Physics Overclaim Audit (On-Screen Copy)
Overall, the copywriting shows **strong scientific restraint** — there are **zero** pop-sci overclaims about particles "talking faster than light," "deciding instantaneously," or "not existing until observed," and Step 7's *"Bob's last 24 flashes (pure coin flips)"* is strictly accurate for a maximally entangled pair. Four smaller wording spots should still be tightened so a physicist or sharp student cannot nitpick them:

1. **Step 0 (`00-first`):** *"Two boxes. No wires. How do they always agree?"* — Mild overstatement; they only *always* agree when both dials match (Step 2 shows ~50% overall). Mentioning *"Same dial, same colour"* or keeping *"when set the same"* prevents a minor contradiction two clicks later.
2. **Step 3 (`03-card-grid`):** *"Because matching dials agreed 100%, both particles must carry the same hidden card."* — Stated unconditionally as a fact about particles before refuting it. Should stay explicitly conditional: *"If hidden cards ran the boxes, 100% agreement on matching dials means both particles would have to carry the same card."*
3. **Step 4 (`04-peel-off`):** *"No wires. No cards. Just tilted magnets."* — Declaring *"No cards"* as settled ontology right as we inspect local magnet hardware skips the no-signaling / independent-choices caveat and leaves open why magnets behave differently from pre-set cards.
4. **Step 6 (`06-breakthrough` & `07-settled-85`):** *"No hidden card — no plan made in advance — can do this. Nature just did."* — Two subtle issues: (a) omitting *"without talking"* (locality) from the punchline lets a sharp student ask *"what if they're secretly communicating?"* (a pre-set plan *plus* communication easily beats 75%); (b) *"Nature just did"* slightly blurs this browser simulation with real-world Bell experiments (which Step 8 is about to introduce — *"Real entangled pairs in nature do"* is cleaner).

### B. Breakthrough Wow-Moment Verdict (`06-breakthrough-shatter`)
- **Does it look like glass shattering?** **Only halfway.** You immediately *understand* that the 75% barrier broke, and the golden beam pushing across to `85.4%` with the lever flipped right reads cleanly. However, visually the burst looks like a **tight vertical clump of opaque matte grey-white paper/origami triangles** rather than transparent, refractive, specular-glinting glass shards. Worse, the top triangles collide with and obscure the `PLAN LIMIT` text on desktop, and on phone (`06-breakthrough-shatter-phone.png`) the entire shatter degrades into a **tiny blurry grey-white smudge**.
- **Would a visitor gasp?** **Not yet.** On desktop, a visitor gets a pleasant *"oh, nice!"* (aided by sound and slow-mo if running live), not a true gasp — partly because the shards feel matte/clumped rather than jewel-like glass catching golden light, and partly because Step 5 already showed `76.3%` above 75% right before the reveal. On phone, the cramped, soft-focus top viewport kills the drama entirely. With glossy translucent shards radiating outward, clean sky framing around the labels, crisp phone DPR, and no `76.3%` anticlimax on Step 5, this beat has everything it needs to become genuinely gasp-worthy.

## 4. Teacher View — Will It Work on a Projector at the Back of a Classroom?

**Verdict: Close on `1280×720`, NOT ready yet on `1920×1080` or in a lit room.**

- **What already works well on `09-teacher-mode-proj1280.png`:**
  - The pedagogical flow is genuinely classroom-ready: the persistent `Rule:` box stops students from forgetting the game rules, stripping `×1 / ×100` down to `Fire 1,000` avoids misclicks, and **`The class has voted — reveal lever`** is a killer peer-instruction mechanic.
  - On `1280×720`, the main headline (`Can any card beat 75%?`), big `75.4%` score, Alice/Bob toggle buttons, `ALICE`/`BOB` plaques, and gold CTA are readable from the back of a typical 8-meter classroom.
- **What fails from the back row (especially on `1080p` projectors or with classroom lights partly on):**
  1. **Insufficient scaling on `1920×1080` (`09-teacher-mode-desk.png`):** Unlike `proj1280`, the 1080p Teacher view only modestly enlarges text; the `Rule:` banner, `STEP 5` eyebrow, `0&0 ✓ / 1&1 ✗` pills, `Hidden card (3/4 = 75%)`, `Chance of luck: still possible`, and `How do we know?` will be unreadable past row 4.
  2. **Low-contrast dark-on-dark surfaces:** Overhead projectors wash out subtle dark-slate cards (`#161B26` vs `#1F2636`), muted grey subtext (`PLAN LIMIT`, `Hidden card`), and dark-tinted `0&0 ✓` / `1&1 ✗` pills whenever room lights aren't fully off.
  3. **Live-class RNG distraction:** Firing 1,000 rounds in `proj1280` produced **`75.4%`** — visibly over 75% — forcing the teacher to derail the lesson to explain sampling noise before the reveal.
  4. **Tiny 3D details:** The `0` / `1` markings on the brass dials and `PLAN LIMIT` text are too small from the back row, and Alice's floating `▲` ring collides with the score bar.
